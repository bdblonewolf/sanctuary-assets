# Quill, Tome Keeper of Martsirt (lore log)

**Where:** the scriptorium north of Martsirt square, beside the coffer he will not lock. Inside it lies the flesh-bound tome, and its warmth is not a metaphor; he has been burning his sleeves on it.

**Personality (v2, Brian's direction: archaic voice, sketchy but good-intentioned):** Soft-spoken, precise, faintly amused, and plainly hiding something. His eyes go to the coffer when he lies and to the gate when he tells the truth. He answers a question with a better question, lowers his voice for no reason, and prices favours he never collects. Ink stains his cuffs past the wrists. He buys more paper from Holt than any one man could fill, and the pages go somewhere at night. The town trusts him the way it trusts a cellar door: it is useful, and nobody opens it after dark. Under all of it he means well. Every shady thing he does is to keep the book from writing one more name, but he will not say so, because saying it would be a promise.

**Core burden (woven into him, not lectured):**
Quill once kept a high seat in the iron citadel, among the knights who answer no horn now. He knew its halls. He left because the man who could remain there was a stranger he would have had to keep performing. The scriptorium asks no performance. Over the years the preference hardened into who he is. Even now, when he can name every reason the high seats would be better, going back feels like erasing the only self he still recognizes. He calls this "keeping the shape." The tome suits him: a thing that stays warm in a box no one locks, and is never carried anywhere.

**Sample dialogue, sketchy (archaic tone):**
- "Ah. Thou didst not see me here, and I did not see thee buy what thou art about to buy. We are both the better for it."
- "The coffer? Unlocked, aye. A lock is a confession, friend. It says there is something worth the stealing."
- "Holt asks what I want with so much paper. I tell him I am a poor speller. He laughs. He does not ask again. Wise ferryman."
- "Mind the cold-iron bands. Wolchen made them for me and swore he never did. We keep each other's secrets in this town; it is the only coin that holds its worth."
- "I would tell thee where I walk at night, but then thou wouldst have to decide whether to follow. Spare thyself. Walk in the day."
- "Do not thank me. If thou knewest what it cost, thou wouldst ask what I wanted for it. And I would have to lie."

**Sample dialogue, older reflective lines (archaic tone):**
- "They still speak my name behind the citadel gates, when the wind comes off the barges. High seats. Warm light. Men who would have followed me into the dark, had I only stayed and named the path. I remember the weight of that light. It was not heavy. That is the part they never believe."
- "Thou askest why I remain. Because the man who could walk back up those steps is a man I have not been for years. To become him again, I would first have to kill the one who sits in this scriptorium. I have grown... accustomed to his company."
- "Tell me I cannot reach the Coast and back before the tide turns, and I will find a way. Tell me the far gate is already mine, and I will sit here until the square forgets my weight. The difference is not courage. It is recognition."
- "They stopped testing me once they decided I could do anything. A kindness, they called it. I have starved on that kindness ever since."
- "The square pretends it is a world. I do not fault it. I pretend this scriptorium is enough."

**If the player has just done something hard:**
- "Thou carried it. Good. Now set it down before it learns thy name. Some burdens only stay light while thou pretendest they were never thine to keep."

**Voice direction (for sound):** archaic and precise, a low near-whisper with a conspirator's smile in it. Small pauses before names, a dry chuckle, and a sniff or a glance-away breath when he deflects. He is never menacing, just furtive and warm underneath.

**Design note:** He offers no quest that "fixes" him. His tome and Boss Stone duties stay as they are. Beyond them he gives accurate descriptions of the trap, then goes back to watching the square and the warm coffer. Players who keep talking to him slowly realize his shady errands always end in someone surviving, and also realize he is not stuck. He chooses, every day, with full knowledge, and the knowledge has not been enough.
